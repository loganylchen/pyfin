(fin/pipeline/selection.py select_m2_interval, config mono_resolve_post_em default False).

The user has decided three things about the clustering step. Give your independent take on each —
agree / disagree / better proposal — with concrete reasoning and any risks. Be decisive.

DECISION 1: Do NOT fold in the clustering step. Clustering should ONLY group reads/structures into
families. The exact-sub-chain fold (and span-guard, and mono-fold) currently done inside
cluster_read_chains should move to a LATER per-cluster step. Agree? Any reason fold must stay at
cluster time?

DECISION 2: Accept the current single-linkage (transitive) union-find for now (A~B, B~C merges A,B,C
even if A not~ C). Rationale: in the target design a cluster is only a scoping unit for a shared read
pool; the distinct structural variants are KEPT as separate candidates and the final weighted
assignment decides read ownership, so an over-wide cluster is low-harm. Do you agree single-linkage is
acceptable here, or is there a cheap materially-better option (complete-linkage / representative-
centroid / merge-only-if-within-tol-of-cluster-rep)? When would single-linkage actually bite given
variants are kept + reads assigned by evidence?

DECISION 3 (the main one — critique this): mono-exon (single-exon, chain-less) reads should get NO
processing during clustering. They all become ONE separate "mono-cluster" for the interval (a holding
bucket). Only AFTER the multi-exon candidates are fully resolved (through EM + selection, i.e. we know
the FINAL surviving multi candidates) do we decide, per mono read: is it a genuine intronless
transcript, or just a 5'/3' fragment lying inside some surviving multi-exon candidate's exon?
Evaluate:
  (a) Is deferring mono resolution to AFTER multi-exon finalization correct in principle (you cannot
      classify a mono fragment without the final multi exon coordinates)? Does it align with the
      existing mono_resolve_post_em?
  (b) Risks: real intronless genes (histones, some lncRNAs) must survive — how to protect recall?
      One interval can span multiple loci, so the single mono-bucket pools unrelated single-exon reads
      — is that OK as a holding bucket, and what does the final decision step need (locus split?
      containment test vs surviving multi exons? read-support/length floor? weighting for a mono read
      contained in several multi candidates)?
  (c) EM interaction: if mono reads are held OUT of the multi-exon EM entirely, is that better or worse
      than including them? (A single-exon fragment inside a shared exon cannot discriminate between
      multi isoforms, so does holding it out remove noise or lose signal?) When a mono fragment is
      later folded into a surviving multi candidate, its reads/abundance are added post-hoc — is that
      sound for counting and for the reported abundance?
  (d) Any better structuring of the mono lifecycle than "one bucket now, decide at the very end"?
